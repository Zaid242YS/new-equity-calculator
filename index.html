What should each participant own?

Equity is the residual ownership of the company after its obligations. It can carry rights to distributions, voting and proceeds on sale, but those rights depend on the company documents and share terms. Your task is to agree what the farm is worth before new investment, what each participant actually contributes, and how future work will be rewarded.

A fair allocation balances capital at risk with an existing, transferable business. The founder may contribute funded assets, operating knowledge, designs, customer relationships and completed development work. Investors provide money needed to build and operate the farm. Managers deliver results over time. These contributions differ in timing, certainty and recoverability; they should not receive identical treatment automatically.

What your September 2026 proposal establishes: it suggests founder 30–40%, investors 40–55%, and management 10–15%. These are proposed negotiation ranges, not appraised market benchmarks. Core tilapia and tomato revenue, final CAPEX and a complete operating budget remain undetermined. This app does not treat the ranges as evidence of value.

1. Separate three meanings of equity

Accounting equity is recorded assets less recorded liabilities. Economic equity value is an estimate of the business’s value to its owners today. Ownership equity is each holder’s percentage of the shares. A company with SAR 1 million of book equity can be negotiated at a different economic value; a 40% shareholder does not automatically receive 40% of annual revenue.

2. Define the transaction before calculating

This app models a primary cash investment into the company with ordinary shares, followed by a new management pool that dilutes founder and investor proportionately. All existing ownership is grouped as “founder / existing holders.” There are no preferred returns, convertible instruments, secondary share purchases or different share classes. If investors buy shares directly from the founder, the company does not receive that cash and the funding analysis must change.

Post-money equity value = pre-money equity value + new investment
Investor share before pool = investment ÷ post-money value
Investor share after pool = investor share before pool × (1 − pool %)
Founder share after pool = pre-money ÷ post-money × (1 − pool %)

For example, a SAR 1,500,000 pre-money value and SAR 1,000,000 investment produce 40% investor and 60% founder ownership before a pool. Creating a 10% pool after that round yields 36% investor, 54% founder and 10% management. The pool reduces both existing allocations by 10% of their former size, not ten percentage points. A pre-money pool can place more dilution on the founder; it is a different deal and is not modeled here.

3. Build a defensible pre-money value

Adjusted net assets: independently value company-owned land, tanks, greenhouses, filtration, pumps, solar equipment, inventories and cash; subtract liabilities and restoration obligations. A founder’s personally owned land is not a company asset until it is validly contributed. For leased land, do not count the full freehold value; evaluate the transferable lease right and obligations. Money already spent and the equipment purchased with that money are not two separate contributions.

Transferable intangibles and completed work: documented designs, exclusive rights, customer contracts and useful completed development may support negotiation value. Record unpaid work as verified hours × reasonable replacement rate × an agreed recognition percentage. This is a negotiation aid, not automatic accounting capitalization. Do not count paid salary again as sweat equity or value the same technical work as both intellectual property and labor. An untested concept or introduction to a potential buyer may justify little immediate value.

Income approach: estimate future free cash flows, ramp-up losses, replacement expenditure and working capital, then discount them at a rate appropriate to the risk. A full discounted cash flow model should reflect each production phase and a defensible terminal value. This app does not perform a DCF.

Earnings cross-check: a multiple of sustainable EBITDA can indicate operating enterprise value. Deduct financing debt and add excess cash to reach equity value. The multiple must come from relevant transactions or a reasoned professional assessment. The app’s multiples are illustrative assumptions, not Saudi aquaculture benchmarks. For a pre-revenue farm, projected mature earnings require explicit allowance for construction, ramp-up and execution risk.

Choose an agreed pre-money amount after reconciling these approaches. Do not add an asset valuation to a full earnings valuation: the assets normally produce the earnings already capitalized. The app shows alternative indications side by side and uses your explicitly entered pre-money value for ownership.

4. Use farm economics to challenge the valuation

Model saleable biomass rather than theoretical tank capacity. Annual tilapia kilograms equal fish stocked per cycle × cycles per year × survival rate × harvest weight. These inputs must match growth duration, tank capacity, stocking density, water quality and a realistic stocking schedule. The app is a steady-state annual model, not a first-year harvest schedule.

Revenue equals saleable kilograms × realized farm-gate price, plus independently supportable crop revenue. Deduct feed, fingerlings, packaging, transport, crop consumables, labor, leases, maintenance, utilities, biosecurity, testing, insurance and administration. Integrated aquaponics can share costs, but vegetables are not cost-free. Backup aeration and pumping, oxygen monitoring and emergency water provision remain costs even with passive greenhouse cooling.

The feed approximation here is saleable fish kilograms × effective feed conversion ratio × feed price. Use an effective ratio that includes actual feed losses and mortality effects. A detailed stocking-cohort model is better where initial fish biomass and late mortality materially affect feed consumed. Fingerling cost is based on all fish stocked, including those that do not survive.

5. Reward future management through vesting

Future service is not an asset delivered today. Reserve a negotiated management pool and vest it as service and measurable milestones are completed, with clear departure and repurchase terms. For example, a four-year arrangement can combine continued service with verified survival, feed efficiency, sales collections and budget performance. Those are proposed commercial terms, not required Saudi standards. The app displays the entire reserved pool on a fully diluted basis even if none has vested; actual voting and distribution rights before vesting require a separate agreement.

6. Price and allocate risk explicitly

Construction overruns, summer heat, low dissolved oxygen, disease, feed inflation, weak collections and lack of buyers can reduce both value and available cash. A larger investor share may reflect a lower valuation and higher risk; it is not a quality score. Pair equity discussions with milestone funding, reporting, spending limits, reserved decisions, related-party controls, capital-call rules and dispute procedures. Avoid adjusting the same risk several times without explanation through a lower forecast, a larger discount rate and a separate haircut.

Keep unvalidated expansion out of the core case. Your proposal labels leafy-green output as an R&D target; its 144 heads × 500 m² × 12 × SAR 1 arithmetic equals SAR 864,000, whereas the narrative rounds to about SAR 840,000. Neither figure is established operating revenue. Alfalfa used internally cannot also be counted as an external sale. This app starts additional crop revenue at zero.

7. Resolve Saudi execution conditions before closing

MISA’s updated investment framework addresses investor registration, and MEWA publishes aquaculture licensing procedures. Verify the specific entity, activity, site, investor status and current permits with the relevant authorities. Investment registration does not replace sector approvals. Have Saudi advisers confirm land ownership or leasing eligibility for the exact Madinah parcel, environmental and water permissions, corporate capital contributions, offering rules and shareholder terms. The app does not certify any of these conditions. For an Islamic equity structure, separately review profit-sharing, loss allocation and guarantees with qualified Shariah and legal advisers.

Recommended negotiation sequence

  1. Confirm the company boundary, land rights and who owns every contributed asset.
  2. Obtain supplier quotations, a phased funding budget and realistic harvest / sales schedules.
  3. Verify biological assumptions through an aquaculture specialist and pilot records.
  4. Agree a pre-money range; document why it differs from asset and earnings indications.
  5. Agree investor amount, pool timing, vesting, votes, distributions and future funding rights.
  6. Complete legal, accounting and Shariah reviews before executing and collecting capital.
